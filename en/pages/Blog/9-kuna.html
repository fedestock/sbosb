Jungle & Culture in San Blas: A Day with the Kuna

A journey to San Blas isn’t complete without stepping off the boat and into the lush jungle — where the rhythms of the land meet the traditions of the Guna people.

If you’re curious about more than beaches and sunsets, this immersive experience will connect you with something deeper: a vibrant, living culture rooted in harmony with nature.

The Adventure Begins

The day starts early, with a scenic ride to the mainland. Your guide, Lisa — a Guna woman born and raised in this jungle — welcomes you into her world with quiet strength and deep knowledge. She leads you on a trail where rivers weave between trees, birds call from the canopy, and hidden waterfalls appear like secrets in the green. This isn’t a manicured park. It’s real. It’s raw. And it’s alive.

A Cultural Encounter

After the hike, you’ll arrive at Lisa’s family home for a traditional lunch — prepared from local ingredients and cooked the Guna way. You’ll eat in an open-air kitchen, share stories, and learn how the forest shapes every part of life: from medicine to food, to myths passed down by the elders.

Lisa doesn’t just teach you about Guna culture — she shows you what it feels like to be part of it, even for a day.

Why People Love This Experience

It’s authentic and personal — no big groups, no tourist buses. It supports local women and traditional knowledge. It’s a chance to see more of Panama than most travelers ever do. You return to the boat not only with muddy shoes but with a new perspective.

How to Join

This experience is available as part of the Jungle Package or Mix Package aboard Thyra. It includes transport, guiding, lunch, and plenty of surprises along the trail.

Our crew consists of a captain and a sailor/cook. Life on a sailboat is very different from hotels or cruises it’s more like a motorhome on the water. We move with both sails and a motor, use solar energy, and have limited fresh water for showers (drinking water is always available). Spaces are minimalist but well organized, and most of the time we wear swimsuits and go barefoot.

Activities are personalized: snorkeling, island exploration, and time with guests, while also allowing moments of solitude. Transfers to the sailboat and the dinghy require some physical effort, so basic swimming and water skills are needed.

We minimize plastic use and ask guests to bring coral-friendly sunscreen.
